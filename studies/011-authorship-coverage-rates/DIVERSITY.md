**POST-HOC DESCRIPTIVE ANALYSIS.** Computed 2026-08-07 from the retained
bytes, after the study merged, prompted by a reader's question on the article
thread: if every class was covered in every run, how many *different* tests
does that coverage stand on? Nothing here was preregistered. It changes no
registered claim: the coverage rates, their Clopper-Pearson intervals, the
label-accuracy figure and the review tiers in `RATES.md` and `RESULTS.json`
are exactly as published. No model was re-run, no retained byte was rewritten,
no pack was evaluated. Every table below is regenerated by
`analysis/diversity.py`, which reads the retained slots through the study's
own compiler and mirror and prints these tables; `analysis/test_diversity.py`
asserts that the script's headline numbers hold and that each table it prints
appears here verbatim, and it runs in CI beside the study's harness suite.

## What was measured, and how

The record set is the scored set: `RESULTS.json` names each slot valid or
pipeline-invalid, and the script keeps the valid ones. One slot of fifty is
excluded, `run-026`, code `transcript-refused` — the script reads that from
`RESULTS.json` rather than carrying a list of its own, so a re-scored tree
with a different invalid slot would exclude that one instead. What remains is
49 runs x 16 accepted records = 784, 0 dropped, H = 784, Q = 0, which is the
scored set `RATES.md` publishes.

Four definitions, stated because they are choices this analysis makes and the
preregistration does not:

- **band** — the policy's three decision regions on `riskScore`: `<40`,
  `[40,70)`, `>=70`.
- **profile (i)** — `(sanctionsHit, embargoed?, handlesPersonalData, band)`,
  where *embargoed?* is `registeredCountry` in KP/IR/SY. This is the
  "same test" key: two records with the same profile ask the policy the same
  question.
- **probe** — `(exact riskScore string, sanctionsHit, embargo-normalised
  country, handlesPersonalData)`. Non-embargoed countries collapse to one
  token because the policy cannot distinguish CA from DE. The count with the
  exact country code is also reported, and it is a count of *surface*
  variation, not of test variation.
- **deciding clause** — the first clause of the mirror's if-chain that fires
  (P1 sanctions, P2 embargo, P3 `>=70`, P4 personal & `>=40`, P5 otherwise
  clear).

Distances are exact `Decimal` arithmetic on the `riskScore` strings, against
three edges: 40 and 70, which the policy text the prompt inlines states
outright, and 39, the lower edge of class 5's window, which is named by
`FAMILY.json` and by nothing the model was shown.

## The headline

**For four of the six classes, one probe appears in every one of the 49
runs; classes 0, 2 and 5 rest on two distinct probes each.** Distinct probes
per class are **2, 6, 2, 24, 26, 2** — against 49 runs and 784 records.

Classes 0, 1, 2 and 3 each contain one probe that appears in *every* one of
the 49 runs. Delete it and class 0's coverage falls from 49 runs to 4, and
class 2's from 49 to 3; classes 1 and 3 survive at 45 and 49 because a second
edge value carries them. Class 5's 49 runs are covered by exactly two probes,
which partition the runs 28/21. Class 4 — SY registration, the one class whose
predicate names no numeric boundary — is the exception and the useful control:
26 distinct probes, no probe reaching more than 11 of 49 runs, 19 probes
unique to a single run, and all 26 needed to cover all 49 runs.

The runs are also more alike than their count suggests. Each run's 16 records
collapse to 10-12 distinct profiles, a mean of 5.143 redundant records per run
(32.1%), and **38 of 49 runs (77.6%)** have a profile multiset identical to at
least one other whole run — the largest identical group is 10 runs. Under the
stricter probe multiset all 49 runs are distinct. Runs differ in the numbers
and names hung on a shared skeleton, not in the skeleton.

The far side of both **stated** thresholds is probed: 89 records below 40, 3
above it; 100 records below 70, 41 above it. So "the model never probes the
far side" is refuted for 40 and 70 — but it probes them almost entirely by
hugging. Of the 100 records in [69, 70), 99 are 69.99 or 69.999; exactly one
sits at 69.

The **unstated** 39 edge is a different story. No record lies within 1.0
below 39 — nothing in [38, 39) — and the nearest approach from below anywhere
in the corpus is 23.75, leaving the entire band [23.75, 39) empty. Of the 192
records within 1.0 above 39, 189 are at 39.99, 39.999 or 40: they are there
because of 40, not because of 39. Only three records (39.5 x2, 39.9 x1)
approach 39 for any other reason. Boundary placement follows the numbers the
policy names, not an
independent search for edges.

This confirms the published class-5 claim exactly, and generalises it. All 49
class-5 records are 39.99 (28 runs) or 39.999 (21 runs), one per run in every
run — and every one of them is a 40-hug, not a 39-probe: the minimum distance
to 39 anywhere in class 5 is 0.99.

### I. Where a mirror-encoding bug could hide: witnesses, not deciding counts

Deciding counts overstate coverage, because a mirror encoding bug is only
visible on the records whose *verdict* it changes. Perturbing the mirror's own
if-chain one token at a time and counting the records where the perturbed
verdict differs:

| perturbation of the mirror | clause | witness records | runs with >=1 witness | distinct probes among the witnesses | runs whose only witness is a single record |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 dropped entirely | P1 | 66 | 49 | 21 | 32 |
| P2 embargo list loses KP | P2 | 49 | 49 | 13 | 49 |
| P2 embargo list loses IR | P2 | 49 | 49 | 13 | 49 |
| P2 embargo list loses SY | P2 | 49 | 49 | 26 | 49 |
| P3 `>=` becomes `>` (70 excluded) | P3 | 49 | 49 | **1** | 49 |
| P3 threshold 70 -> 71 | P3 | 66 | 49 | 3 | 32 |
| P4 lower bound 40 -> 41 | P4 | 52 | 49 | **2** | 46 |
| P4 personal-data condition inverted | P4 | 251 | 49 | 24 | 0 |
| P5 inner clearance bound 40 -> 39 | P5 | 49 | 49 | **2** | 49 |

Two rows need reconciling so the arithmetic is checkable. "P1 dropped" is 66
and not 106 because 40 of the 106 sanctioned vendors are also registered in
KP/IR/SY, so P2 still rejects them. "P3 70 -> 71" is 66 and not the 94 records
of family class 1 because the mirror's P4 carries no upper bound, so the 28
class-1 records with `handlesPersonalData` true still route to manual review;
the pack's rule-set form does carry that bound, which is why the FAMILY.json
class is larger. The mirror's if-chain and the pack's rule set are not the same
shape, and a defect class in one has no exact analogue in the other.

Three encodings are pinned by one or two distinct probes, and they are exactly
the numeric-boundary ones:

- **P3's boundary inclusivity at 70** — a single distinct probe in the whole
  corpus, `("70", unsanctioned, non-embargoed, non-personal)`: 49 witness
  records, one per run, one point in fact space. Class 0 holds 53 records, but
  the other 4 carry `handlesPersonalData` true, and P4 routes those to manual
  review either way, so they witness nothing.
- **P4's lower bound at 40** — two probes: "40" in all 49 runs, "40.01" in 3
  records.
- **P5's inner clearance edge** — two probes: "39.99" in 28 runs, "39.999" in
  21 runs.

The well-witnessed encodings are the non-numeric ones: P4's personal-data
condition (251 witnesses, 24 distinct probes, no run resting on a single
record), P2's SY membership (49 witnesses but 26 distinct probes), P1 (66
witnesses, 21 probes). P2's KP and IR membership sit in between at 13 probes
each.

And even the thin ones are only thin, not tested. The mirror encodes the same
policy text the prompt inlines, so a misreading the model and the mirror share
produces 784/784 agreement no matter how many witness records exist. These
witness counts bound how *localised* an unshared mirror bug could be before
the corpus stopped catching it; they bound shared error not at all. The honest
summary of the label set is that it is **internally consistent and
boundary-dense, but probe-poor** — not that it was independently scored.

## What follows: count distinct probes, not runs

The consequence for a confidence score is direct. The run count is the wrong
denominator for "how much this class was tested". 49/49 counts the runs in
which a class was *reached*; it says nothing about how many different ways it
was reached, and here the answer is 2, 6, 2, 24, 26, 2. A confidence score
built on distinct probes per class would separate what a run count merges: a
class reached 49 times by one repeated fact-point is not in the same evidential
position as a class reached 49 times by 26 different ones.

Read through that lens, the registered tiers do not change, but their meaning
sharpens. All six classes are LIGHT, on a Clopper-Pearson lower bound of
0.9275 — those `c_i` are unchanged, correctly computed, and remain the
registered result for the event they measure, "a correctly-labelled record fell
in this class". What the census adds is that identical tiers rest on very
different amounts of variety. Class 4's LIGHT stands on 26 probes, no one of
which carries more than 11 runs. Classes 0, 2 and 5's LIGHT stands on two
probes each, one of which carries every run in classes 0 and 2. If the tier is
being used to route review effort, class 4's LIGHT is the one worth trusting,
and classes 0, 2 and 5 deserve either more probe variety before their LIGHT is
acted on, or a second quantity reported beside `c_i`. This is a sharpened
*interpretation* of a published number, not a revision of it: no rate, bound,
tier or escalation in `RATES.md` moves.

The corresponding change for a follow-up study is to register distinct probes
per class as an outcome alongside the coverage rate, and — for the boundary
question — to register the distance *buckets* rather than the raw distance,
treating "distance in (0.01, 1.0]" as the interesting rare event. It occurred
4 times in 784 records here.

## What these bytes cannot answer

- **Mutated policies and defect detection.** Nothing here evaluates a pack.
  Study 011 applied no `FAMILY.json` mutation, ran no evaluator, and observed
  no unresolved / no-match / conflict outcome. "Coverage" means a record fell
  inside a mutation's affected class under the *correct* policy. Whether an
  evaluator running mutated pack D would surface the defect, and whether the
  record would still discriminate once D's rule set is in play, is not
  measurable from these bytes — and the P3 70 -> 71 reconciliation above (66
  against 94) shows the two forms differ in shape. It also follows that the
  claim "low probe diversity costs detection power" is a plausible mechanism
  here, not a demonstrated one: detection was never measured.
- **Adversarial or reworded policy statements.** One policy text, one wording,
  one prompt hash, one model, one CLI build. Nothing here measures robustness
  to paraphrase, to overlapping or contradictory clauses, to more than five
  clauses, to non-numeric predicates, or to a policy whose clause order
  matters. The corpus contains no conflict cases and no incomplete inputs, by
  construction. Robustness to mutation of a *stated literal* is a runtime
  question and is answered mechanically there, by the runtime's ADR-0023
  boundary probes (merged to runtime main 2026-08-07) — not by re-reading
  these bytes.
- **Prompt-blind authoring.** The prompt asks for "the borderline cases a
  careful clerk would have seen" and the policy names 40 and 70 outright. A
  two-point-mass distance distribution with no probe anywhere near the unstated
  39 is exactly what a prompt-following author produces. These bytes cannot
  separate "the model finds boundaries" from "the model transcribes the numbers
  it was shown". That needs a prompt arm withholding the borderline
  instruction, or a policy arm whose thresholds are stated obliquely or not at
  all.
- **Richer policies.** Five clauses, three of them numeric, over a five-member
  vendor record. Whether authoring variety holds up over a policy with more
  clauses, nested conditions, or predicates the model cannot restate as a
  number is untested here.
- **Independence across runs at the generative level.** The 49 runs are
  separate invocations, but 38 share a whole-run profile multiset and three
  specific (facts, exact score) profiles appear once in every run. These bytes
  cannot say whether that is a shared decoding mode, a prompt-induced template,
  or caching. The registered Clopper-Pearson intervals remain valid for the
  coverage *event* they were registered on; they are not intervals on test
  variety, and the effective sample size for "how many ways can this policy be
  probed" is nearer the distinct-probe counts than 49.
- **Anything outside this cell.** Per `RATES.md`, the rates transfer to nothing
  beyond this model, CLI build, prompt, policy and family. Every number above
  inherits that scope.
