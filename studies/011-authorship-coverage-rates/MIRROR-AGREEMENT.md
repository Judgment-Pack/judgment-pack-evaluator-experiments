# Was the mirror read once or twice? — a clean-room second scorer

**POST-HOC DESCRIPTIVE ANALYSIS.** Computed 2026-08-07 from the retained
bytes, after the study merged, in answer to the census's own caveat. Nothing
here was preregistered. It changes no registered claim: the coverage rates,
their Clopper-Pearson intervals, the label-accuracy figure and the review
tiers in `RATES.md` and `RESULTS.json` are exactly as published. No model was
re-run, no retained byte was rewritten, no pack was evaluated. Every table
below is regenerated by `analysis/agreement.py`, which puts both mirrors to
the retained records and to a constructed grid and exits nonzero on any
disagreement; `analysis/test_agreement.py` pins its counts and this document's
labels, and it runs in CI beside the study's harness suite.

## What a clean-room second mirror is, and why this study needed one

`DIVERSITY.md` ends its witness analysis with a caveat that no amount of
authored variety can repair:

> The mirror encodes the same policy text the prompt inlines, so a misreading
> the model and the mirror share produces 784/784 agreement no matter how many
> witness records exist. These witness counts bound how *localised* an
> unshared mirror bug could be before the corpus stopped catching it; they
> bound shared error not at all. The honest summary of the label set is that it
> is **internally consistent and boundary-dense, but probe-poor** — not that it
> was independently scored.

That is a circularity, and it has a specific shape. The study's H/Q split, the
coverage classes, and every rate built on them all pass through one function,
`harness/policy_mirror.py:verdict`, written by the same author, from the same
policy text, in the same sitting as the rest of the harness. If that function
misread a clause, the misreading is invisible from inside: the records were
authored against the same text, so they would agree with it, and the census
would report exactly the numbers it reports.

A clean-room second mirror is the cheapest instrument that can see the
circularity from outside. A second author is given the policy text and nothing
else — not the first mirror, not its tests, not a single record — and writes
the function again. Where the two implementations agree, the reading survives
a second pass by an author who could not have copied it. Where they diverge,
the divergence is a place the text underdetermines, or a place one of them is
wrong.

This is a *second scorer*, in the sense that word carries in inter-rater work:
it does not establish that the reading is correct, only that it is reproducible
from the same source by an independent reader. What it cannot do is the last
section of this document.

## Provenance

`analysis/mirror2.py` was written by an agent whose only input was the bytes of
`studies/010-blinded-oracle/policy/POLICY.md` — the same policy text Study
011's authoring prompt inlines.

The isolation rule the builder ran under, stated as it was given: read
`POLICY.md` and nothing else; do not open, read, grep, or list any other file
in any study directory. Its own report closes with what it did in fact
consult — "No other file in any study directory was opened, listed, or
grepped. … No existing test, record, or `FAMILY.json` was consulted." Isolation
of an agent is a claim about a process, not a proof, so it is worth being
precise about what rests on it: if the builder had cheated and copied the
study's mirror, the result below would be a tautology rather than a

the isolation rule and the transcript are recorded rather than the agreement
being reported alone.

boundary cases derived from the text — precedence overrides, `"kp"` as

### The twelve interpretive decisions, in two classes

`MIRROR2-NOTES.md` separates what the text decided from what the author
decided. The split is the useful part of the artifact: it is exactly the list a
reviewer would otherwise have to reconstruct by reading the code.

**Determined by the text (1–6)** — recorded so the reading is auditable, not
because a choice was made.

| # | decision | the words it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | strict cascade P1 → P2 → P3 → P4 → P5 with early return | "regardless of anything else"; "absent a sanctions hit"; "absent a sanctions hit or an embargoed registration" |
| 2 | P3 inclusive at 70 (`risk >= 70`) | "70 or above" |
| 3 | P4 band `[40, 70)`, upper bound carried by P3 having returned | "40 or above but below 70" |
| 4 | P5 as the fall-through residual, not a re-tested condition | P5's rule and its own gloss describe the same leftover set; the trailer's exhaustiveness claim |
| 5 | embargo membership exact and case-sensitive, no `.upper()`, no `.strip()`, no aliasing | "the policy consults no registry — … exact, case-sensitive string comparison against KP, IR, and SY, and any other two-letter code is a non-embargoed registration" |
| 6 | exact decimal arithmetic via `decimal.Decimal`; `float` refused rather than coerced | "risk scores are decimal strings and compare numerically" |

**Left undetermined by the text — the author's choice (7–12)**, of which
three are flagged in the build report as ambiguities, in two distinct kinds:
places the text is *silent*, and places the text *declines to govern*.

| # | decision | kind |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | booleans read by plain truthiness rather than `is True` | ambiguity — the text is **silent**; identical over the declared bool domain, so `1` or `"false"` would count as a hit outside it |
| 8 | missing or malformed members raise (`KeyError`, `InvalidOperation`) rather than defaulting or inventing a fourth outcome | ambiguity — the text **declines to govern**: "the policy takes no position on incomplete inputs" |
| 9 | `riskScore` also accepts `int` and `Decimal`; `bool` refused (it is an `int` subclass) | choice; both accepted types are exact, so no verdict can move |
| 10 | `Decimal`'s wider string grammar accepted as-is, so `"1e2"`, `"+40"`, `"NaN"`, `"Infinity"` parse | ambiguity — the grammar is **undefined** by the text; a stricter regex judged an addition to the policy rather than an implementation of it |
| 11 | score sign and range unbounded — `"-5"` clears, `"1000"` is manual review | choice; nothing in the text caps or floors a score |
| 12 | no high-score reject clause | choice, and a resisted one: P3 has no upper cutoff, so rejection is reachable only via P1 and P2 |

Decision 10 is the one that turned out to be wrong about its own code. See
"Divergences outside the admitted domain".

## What the agreement is worth, clause by clause

784 records are not 784 pieces of evidence. A record only tests an encoding if
a wrong encoding would change its verdict, so the honest measure is the witness
count of `DIVERSITY.md` §I's nine one-token perturbations — scored here over
both instruments at once.

| perturbation of the mirror | clause | corpus witnesses | corpus distinct probes | grid witness cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 dropped entirely | P1 | 66 | 21 | 30 |
| P2 embargo list loses KP | P2 | 49 | 13 | 30 |
| P2 embargo list loses IR | P2 | 49 | 13 | 0 |
| P2 embargo list loses SY | P2 | 49 | 26 | 0 |
| P3 `>=` becomes `>` (70 excluded) | P3 | 49 | 1 | 1 |
| P3 threshold 70 -> 71 | P3 | 66 | 3 | 2 |
| P4 lower bound 40 -> 41 | P4 | 52 | 2 | 2 |
| P4 personal-data condition inverted | P4 | 251 | 24 | 10 |
| P5 inner clearance bound 40 -> 39 | P5 | 49 | 2 | 4 |

Read across the rows, the two instruments are complements, and each is weak
exactly where the other is strong.

**Where the corpus is the evidence.** Precedence, membership and the
personal-data condition rest on 13–26 distinct probes each. Agreement there is
a real second reading: P1 outranking P2/P3/P4 (66 witnesses over 21 probes —
66 and not 106 because 40 of the sanctioned vendors are also KP/IR/SY, which
P2 rejects anyway), the embargo list's three literals, and P4's condition as
distinct from its threshold (251 witnesses, 24 probes, no run resting on a
single record). IR and SY membership is the one place the corpus is the
*stronger* instrument outright: the grid was specified with a single embargoed
code, so those two literals have zero grid witnesses and their agreement rests
wholly on the corpus's 13 and 26 probes.

**Where the corpus is close to vacuous.** Every numeric boundary. P3's
inclusivity at 70 is pinned by exactly ONE distinct probe in the whole
corpus — `("70", unsanctioned, non-embargoed, non-personal)` — replicated 49
times, once per run. (Class 0 holds 53 records, but the other 4 carry
`handlesPersonalData` true and P4 routes those to manual review either way, so
they witness nothing.) P4's floor at 40 has two probes, `"40"` in all 49 runs
and `"40.01"` in 3 records; P5's inner edge has two, `"39.99"` in 28 runs and
`"39.999"` in 21. Strip the redundancy and the corpus's entire boundary
evidence is about **five points** — 70, 40, 40.01, 39.99, 39.999. The other
779 records re-ask questions already asked.

**What the grid adds, and what it cannot.** The 120 cells catch seven of the
nine perturbations, including `>= 70` → `> 70` and `40` → `41`, which the
corpus catches only by the accident of one authored value. Two honest limits.
First, the `>= 70` vs `> 70` discrimination has exactly one witness cell in the
grid too — an equality boundary is a single point in fact space, and no amount
of authoring or gridding changes that. What the grid supplies is that the
point is placed *by construction* rather than by the model's luck, plus
near-side/far-side pairs (39/39.5/39.99/39.999 against 40/40.01/41, and
69/69.99 against 70/70.001/71) and values like 39 and 41 that the corpus never
authors at all — per `DIVERSITY.md` §B there is nothing in [38, 39). Second,
one embargoed code means the grid says nothing about IR and SY. A follow-up
grid should carry all three codes plus a lowercase and a three-letter control.

## Divergences outside the admitted domain

The mirrors are not extensionally identical *everywhere*. Two real behavioural
divergences exist, and both sit outside anything the study can admit.

**`float` and `bool` `riskScore`.** `MIRROR2-NOTES.md` decisions 6 and 9 refuse
both with a `TypeError`, on the grounds that binary coercion is the exact
failure mode the trailer's exact-arithmetic requirement exists to prevent. The
study's mirror hands whatever it is given straight to `Decimal`, which accepts a
float and constructs from its binary value, and accepts `True` as
`Decimal('1')`. Confirmed: `riskScore=69.99` (float) gives study
`manual-review` against clean `TypeError`; `riskScore=True` gives study `clear`
against clean `TypeError`.

Stated precisely, this is unreachable through the pipeline, and the claim is
checked rather than asserted — `analysis/agreement.py` §E puts each value to
`records_compile.classify` inside an otherwise well-formed record:

| riskScore put to the compiler | admitted? | drop code |
|---|---|---|
| 69.99 (str, the control) | yes | — |
| 69.99 (float) | **no** | schema |
| 40.0 (float) | **no** | schema |
| True (bool) | **no** | schema |
| "NaN" | **no** | decimal-form |
| "Infinity" | **no** | decimal-form |
| "1e2" | **no** | decimal-form |
| "+40" | **no** | decimal-form |
| "-5" | **no** | decimal-form |

Floats and bools never reach either mirror because `classify` requires
`isinstance(vendor["riskScore"], str)` and drops anything else with code
`schema`. The exotic decimal spellings never reach either mirror because the
compiler's decimal-string grammar is a canonical `fullmatch` against
`(0|[1-9][0-9]*)(\.[0-9]*[1-9])?` — no sign, no exponent, no leading zero, no
trailing fractional zero, no `NaN`, no `Infinity` — and drops the rest with
code `decimal-form`. Every admitted record in the corpus passed both gates, so
no record in the 784 can present any of these. The divergence matters for one
thing only: the study's mirror is the more permissive of the two and is the
wrong one to lift out of the harness for reuse elsewhere.

**The clean-room notes were wrong about their own code, and the check caught
it.** `MIRROR2-NOTES.md` decision 10 flags — as a consequence it says it is
surfacing rather than hiding — that a `"NaN"` score "compares false against
every bound and would therefore fall through to `clear`". That is false of
`mirror2.py`. `Decimal('NaN') >= Decimal(70)` raises `decimal.InvalidOperation`
in Python; it does not compare false. `mirror2` raises, and so does the study's
mirror, identically:

    study=raise:InvalidOperation  clean=raise:InvalidOperation

Saying this plainly: the mirrors agree here; it is the *documentation* of the
clean-room mirror that is wrong, and a reader trusting decision 10 as written
would believe an unvalidated score silently clears when in fact it explodes.
The note is left in place, uncorrected, because it is the evidence; the
correction lives here and in `analysis/agreement.py` §E, which fails if the
two mirrors ever stop raising the same exception on `"NaN"`. The general
lesson is small and cheap: an author's stated reading of their own code is a
claim like any other, and running it is what settles the claim.

Beyond behaviour, four differences of *posture* are worth recording because
they are what a reviewer would notice. `mirror2` quotes each POLICY.md clause
beside the code implementing it and separates "determined by the text" from "my
choice"; the study's mirror makes every one of the same operative choices —
cascade order, `>= 70`, `>= 40` with the upper bound carried by control flow,
tuple membership with no normalisation, `Decimal` parsing, P5 as a bare
fall-through — and records none of them. Its non-normalisation is a documented
commitment in one and an accident of implementation in the other, so a future
edit adding `.upper()` would read as a fix rather than as a policy change. Its
error behaviour on missing keys agrees by different routes: one reasoned, one
unconsidered. And the study's `EMBARGO` tuple is shared with
`predicate_matches`, the FAMILY.json class matcher in the same file, so one
edit to the embargo list silently moves both the verdict function and the
coverage-class matcher — the two things the study needs to keep independent.
Identical behaviour, non-comparable auditability.

## What this upgrades, and what it does not

**Upgraded.** `DIVERSITY.md`'s summary of the label set was "internally
consistent and boundary-dense, but probe-poor — not that it was independently
scored". The second half of that sentence no longer holds for the clause
encodings. The label set is now *independently reproduced over the corpus and
an exhaustive grid within the admitted domain*: a second author, given only the
policy text, wrote a function that cannot be distinguished from the study's on
any input `records_compile` will pass. The reading of precedence, of the
embargo literals, of P4's condition, of the P5 residual, and of both stated
thresholds' inclusivity survived a second pass.

**Not upgraded — the shared-text ceiling stands.** Both mirrors read the same
`POLICY.md`. A misreading BOTH make remains invisible, and nothing in this
document narrows that. If the text's "40 or above but below 70" is not what the
policy's owner meant, two independent readers of that text will encode the same
wrong thing and agree perfectly with each other and with 784 records authored
from the same text. Agreement measures reproducibility of a reading, never its
correctness. Only two things can break that ceiling: a different POLICY author —
someone stating what the policy *should* do without seeing this wording — or
real-world outcomes, cases decided by the people the policy governs and
compared against the mirrors afterwards. Neither is a code artifact, and
neither is in this study.

Two smaller residuals, neither a defect in the study's scored result. IR and SY
membership is witnessed by no grid cell, so that agreement rests wholly on the
corpus. And the isolation of the clean-room build is a process claim, evidenced
by the builder's transcript and its own report, not proved by the outputs.

## The follow-up ledger, dispositioned

The census closed with open questions. Their state as of 2026-08-07:

**(a) Boundary distance and duplicates — measured, closed.** `DIVERSITY.md`
carries both: every record's distance to the nearest of {39, 40, 70} in
buckets (410 of 784 on a threshold or within 0.01; exactly 4 in the human-sized
gap (0.01, 1.0]), and duplicates at four levels (725 distinct vendor tuples,
373 distinct fact tuples, 17 semantic profiles, 782 of 784 records in a profile
that occurs more than once). Nothing further is owed here.

**(b) A second scorer — done, this document.** 784/784 and 120/120 against a
clean-room mirror built from `POLICY.md` alone. The census's independence
caveat is answered as far as a second reading of the same text can answer it.

**(c) Robustness to mutation of a stated literal — mechanical, closed
elsewhere.** This is a runtime question, not a corpus question, and the runtime
answers it: ADR-0023's boundary probes, merged to runtime main 2026-08-07.
Re-reading these bytes adds nothing to it.

**(d) Semantic and adversarial rewording — genuinely open.** Not answered here
and not answerable from these bytes; a clean-room mirror of the *same* text is
precisely not a test of a different text. If it is ever run, the registered
shape is fixed now so the result cannot be shopped for afterwards: the
**denamed-anchor cell** — the same policy rules, the same family, the same
model and CLI, with the stated literals removed from the prompt (thresholds
described obliquely — "the review threshold", "the personal-data floor" —
rather than as 70 and 40). Registered prediction, directional and pre-stated:
**coverage of the denamed classes collapses**, because placement follows named
numbers. The census's own evidence is why — the corpus hugs both stated
thresholds (410 of 784 records sit on a threshold or within 0.01 of one) and
never approaches the
unstated 39 edge at all, with nothing in [38, 39) and a nearest approach from
below of 23.75. If coverage does *not* collapse, that finding is the
interesting one, and the prediction above is what makes it interesting.

**(e) The axis the study's own analysis names first — policy richness.**
`DIVERSITY.md`'s "what these bytes cannot answer" puts it plainly: five
clauses, three of them numeric, over a five-member record. Whether authoring
variety holds up over a policy with more clauses, nested conditions, or
predicates the model cannot restate as a number is untested, and it is the
first axis to move — a richer policy changes what "a distinct probe" even
means, which no amount of work on this policy can reveal.

**(f) Priority, per the direction.** The recorded-decisions path — transcription
from real decision records — outranks further refinement of the fallback
authoring path. Everything above (a)–(e) refines a fallback: authored records
standing in for decisions nobody actually made. The transcription line tests
the thing the product is for. Work on this study's corpus should stop at
diminishing returns, which this document is close to.
