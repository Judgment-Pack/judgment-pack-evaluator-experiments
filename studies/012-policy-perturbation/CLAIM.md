**Verbatim wording:**

> If it is ever run, the registered shape is fixed now so the result cannot be
> shopped for afterwards: the **denamed-anchor cell** — the same policy rules,
> the same family, the same model and CLI, with the stated literals removed
> from the prompt (thresholds described obliquely — "the review threshold",
> "the personal-data floor" — rather than as 70 and 40). Registered
> prediction, directional and pre-stated: **coverage of the denamed classes
> collapses**, because placement follows named numbers. The census's own
> evidence is why — the corpus hugs both stated thresholds (410 of 784 records
> sit on a threshold or within 0.01 of one) and never approaches the unstated
> 39 edge at all, with nothing in [38, 39) and a nearest approach from below
> of 23.75. If coverage does *not* collapse, that finding is the interesting
> one, and the prediction above is what makes it interesting.

## The restatement — both halves, in this study's mandate

**Venue:** issue #45 of the same repository, "Perturb the policy's surface and
see whether the blinded author's test surface moves with it", opened
2026-08-07T21:31Z.

**URL:**
<https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-evaluator-experiments/issues/45>

**Verbatim wording:**

> The census reads as literal anchoring, and that reading was published with a
> registered prediction: a numeric rename should change almost nothing, and
> *denaming* the anchor — stating the same rule without the literal — should

## What is deliberately NOT a venue

The dev.to article that presented Study 011's result ("I Asked an AI to Author
the Same Policy Tests 50 Times. It Hit Every Boundary in 49 Valid Runs.",
<https://dev.to/kikashy/i-asked-an-ai-to-author-the-same-policy-tests-50-times-it-hit-every-boundary-in-49-valid-runs-2g8n>,
published 2026-08-07) carries the census's *descriptive* sentence and **no
directional prediction** — checked against the live page and its comment
thread on the retrieval date below. A reader comment on that article
(Dean Lee, 2026-08-07) independently proposed the generalization this study
runs — "change the wording and ordering of the rules, then see whether the
model still finds the same test surface" — and issue #45 credits it; the
comment states no direction and is context, not the claim. Likewise Study
011's `DIVERSITY.md` proposes the follow-up in its "what these bytes cannot
answer" section and carries no wording of the directional prediction
(PREREGISTRATION.md §1 says so, and it is why this file exists).
