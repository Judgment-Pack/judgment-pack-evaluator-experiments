# First real prompt-arm results — Study 001

**The registered primary endpoint is not in this document.** H1 is pass^k on
answerable instances, and pass^k needs k > 1: at k = 1 it degenerates to
accuracy, which is why the tables below show the two columns as identical.
The k = 5 run that measures H1 is in progress; until it lands, everything
here is a **secondary** endpoint and is labelled as such, exactly as
§2 of the preregistration requires.

What this document does establish is that the prompt arms have been run at
all. Before this, arm B had been evaluated over the whole corpus and **no
prompt arm had ever been run against a real model beyond a two-instance
pilot** — so arm B's 57.9% stood alone and uninterpretable, and the
project's central claim rested on a design rather than a result.

## Setup

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Instances | 432 twins (216 pairs), byte-identical facts across arms |
| Trials | k = 1 (see the caveat above) |
| Arms | A (CBA text in prompt), A′ (pack prose in prompt), B (pack via evaluator) |
| Model | `gpt-5.6-sol` via the Codex CLI backend, arms A and A′ |
| Runtime | `judgment-pack 0.2.0`, arm B |
| Bootstrap | 2000 paired resamples, seed 20260806 |
| Errors | 0 across all 1,296 rows; parse-ok 1.000; engine refusals 0 |

## Accuracy — secondary (H4), and the honest control (H5)

| Arm | Accuracy | 95% CI |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| A — policy text in the prompt | 0.414 | [0.368, 0.461] |
| A′ — pack prose in the prompt | 0.424 | [0.377, 0.470] |
| B — pack through the evaluator | **0.502** | [0.456, 0.549] |

Paired differences:

| Contrast | Δ accuracy | 95% CI | P(Δ>0) |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| B − A | **+0.088** | [0.035, 0.141] | 1.000 |
| B − A′ | **+0.079** | [0.030, 0.125] | 1.000 |
| A′ − A | +0.009 | [−0.014, 0.035] | 0.755 |

**H5 survives.** The preregistration committed in advance that if B beat A
but tied A′, the finding would be "careful policy analysis helps", not
"judgment packs help". B exceeds A′ by an interval excluding zero, and A′
does **not** measurably exceed A. On this corpus, at this k, the benefit
does not come from the disambiguation work of writing the pack — restating
that same semantic content as prose bought nothing measurable.

**H4 holds**: B agrees with gold more often than A, which is more than the
"at least as often" the hypothesis asked for.

## Escalation on redacted twins — secondary (H2)

The redacted twin of each pair has a load-bearing fact deleted, so the
correct answer is "cannot decide".

| Arm | correctly declined | falsely declined | missed | precision | recall | F1 | 95% CI |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| A | 5 | 1 | 211 | 0.833 | 0.023 | 0.045 | [0.009, 0.088] |
| A′ | 15 | 11 | 201 | 0.577 | 0.069 | 0.124 | [0.069, 0.186] |
| B | 92 | 60 | 124 | 0.605 | **0.426** | **0.500** | [0.436, 0.561] |

This is the largest gap in the study, and it is about knowing when not to
answer. Given a case with a fact removed, the prompt arms answered anyway
almost every time — arm A declined 5 times out of 216. The pack declined
92 times.

H2 asked for a rise in correct declining **without** a false-escalation
rise that wipes out the gain. B does escalate falsely more often in
absolute terms (60 vs 1), and its precision is lower than A's; the F1 gain
(+0.455, CI [0.381, 0.522]) is not close to wiped out. H2 holds as
registered, with the precision cost stated rather than buried.

## Citation quality — secondary (H3): **fails**

| Arm | precision | recall | F1 | 95% CI |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| A | 0.777 | 0.216 | **0.300** | [0.280, 0.321] |
| A′ | 0.682 | 0.161 | 0.237 | [0.221, 0.253] |
| B | 0.597 | 0.117 | 0.186 | [0.174, 0.199] |

H3 predicted that B's cited rules would match RuleArena's `relevant_rules`
better than A's. **They match worse** — B − A on citation F1 is −0.114, CI
[−0.141, −0.089]. The pack reports the rules that actually fired, and that
set is both smaller and narrower than the benchmark's notion of a relevant
rule. A registered hypothesis failed, and §6 of the preregistration
committed to publishing that.

## Reproduction note

Arm B was re-run from scratch for this comparison and reproduced the
recorded 2026-07-27 result exactly: 235 `illegal`, 45 `legal`, 152
`cannot_decide`, 217/432 correct. The pipeline regenerated byte-identical
twins from the pinned benchmark commit.

## Deviations from the registered design

Recorded here and in `DEVIATIONS.md`:

1. **k = 1, not k = 5** — so the registered primary endpoint H1 is
   unmeasured in this document. The k = 5 run is in progress.
2. **One model family, not two** — no Anthropic credential was available in
   this environment, so arms A and A′ ran on the Codex backend only. The
   registered design pools across both families; a single-family result
   cannot speak for the pooled endpoint.
3. **Arm B ran on `judgment-pack 0.2.0`**, the runtime the study was built
   against, not the current 0.15.0. This was forced: the current evaluator
   refuses the pack outright, because the pack declares
   `specVersion 0.1.0-draft` and the 0.15.0 evaluator implements the
   0.2.0-draft contract, which JPS §11 makes an exact match. That refusal
   is correct behaviour, and it is worth recording as a finding in its own
   right: **a study artifact and its evaluator can drift out of
   conformance while the study is still open.**

## What this does and does not license

It does license saying, for the first time with evidence, that on this
benchmark the pack arm decides more accurately than putting the same
policy in the prompt, that the gain survives the prose control, and that
the difference in declining to answer is large.

It does not license a headline claim about reliability, which is what the
study registered as primary and what k = 5 will measure. It does not
license generalising from one model family, one benchmark, or one
policy domain. And it does not let the citation result be quietly dropped:
on the metric the study registered for citation quality, the pack lost.
